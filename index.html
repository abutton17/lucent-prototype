Active management required — autoimmune pathway + alcohol self-medication
ACE 2 is modest, but Hashimoto's thyroiditis is a documented ACE-associated autoimmune condition (stress-immune dysregulation pathway). The key clinical signal here is the alcohol use: 14 drinks/week exceeds NIAAA guidelines and, in the ACE framework, is a textbook stress self-medication pattern (Felitti 1998: 7.4× odds of alcoholism at ACE ≥4; lower ACE still carries elevated risk). Addressing the alcohol without addressing the underlying stress biology is likely to fail. Yellow plan appropriate: active management needed, but not the multi-system crisis of a Red presentation.

1
Safety and stabilization — address alcohol and autoimmune foundations
Alcohol reduction is the first clinical priority. Stress management is the mechanism — not willpower. Autoimmune inflammation responds directly to stress reduction.
Goals: alcohol within NIAAA guidelines · Hashimoto's flare frequency reducing · patient understands stress-autoimmune link · alternative stress coping in place
L1
Brief motivational intervention — alcohol as stress coping
ScienceFelitti 1998 identified alcohol use as a primary ACE coping mechanism — not a standalone substance disorder. Motivational interviewing (MI) framed around stress management (not abstinence) has stronger outcomes for this profile than traditional addiction framing. Goal: reduce to ≤7 drinks/week (NIAAA guidelines for women) while building alternative stress regulation tools.
ActionUse FRAMES model in session (Feedback, Responsibility, Advice, Menu, Empathy, Self-efficacy). Referral to therapist trained in MI if score ≥8. Do not recommend abstinence-only programs — harm reduction framing with stress reframe is more effective for this profile.
Motivational interviewing: L1 RCT; FRAMES model: L2; Felitti 1998 alcohol-ACE link: L1
L2
Anti-inflammatory nutrition — autoimmune support
ScienceHashimoto's is driven by Th1/Th2 immune imbalance exacerbated by chronic cortisol and inflammatory diet. Mediterranean and AIP (autoimmune protocol) dietary patterns reduce autoimmune antibody levels and symptom burden. Also reduces the metabolic impact of alcohol use.
ActionRefer to RD with autoimmune experience. Key priorities: eliminate gluten if not already done (associated with Hashimoto's flares in some patients), increase omega-3s, reduce refined carbohydrates. Selenium supplementation has L2 evidence for reducing TPO antibodies.
Diet + Hashimoto's: L2 systematic review; selenium + TPO: L2 RCT; Nakazawa 2015 autoimmune pathway
L1
Sleep hygiene — alcohol disrupts deep sleep architecture
ScienceAlcohol suppresses REM sleep and reduces slow-wave sleep, causing rebound insomnia that drives further drinking. Addressing sleep disruption directly reduces alcohol use in ~30% of patients without any other intervention. It also reduces cortisol — directly beneficial for autoimmune management.
ActionConsistent wake time every day. No alcohol within 3 hours of sleep. If insomnia persists after alcohol reduction: prescribe CBT-I (Sleepio/SleepStation). Do not prescribe sleep medication — contraindicated with alcohol use.
CBT-I: L1 RCT; alcohol + sleep architecture: L1; sleep + cortisol: L2

L1
TF-CBT or EMDR — flight-primary, cortical window available
ScienceDevelopmental timing ages 7–11 (cortical baseline, 1.0× weight) means the prefrontal cortex was online during the primary ACE period — making cognitive-narrative approaches more accessible than in earlier developmental trauma. Flight-primary presentation (anxiety, over-functioning, hypervigilance) is well-matched to TF-CBT's cognitive restructuring. EMDR remains a valid option if somatic presentation is prominent.
ActionRefer to therapist with TF-CBT training and alcohol/substance experience. Brief on: flight presentation, emotional abuse history, autoimmune context. Expect 12–16 sessions. Ensure therapist is aware of levothyroxine — thyroid fluctuation can affect mood and is sometimes mistaken for therapy regression.
TF-CBT: L1 meta-analysis; EMDR: L1 APA/WHO endorsed; Perry NMT developmental timing rationale: L3
3
Integration — build adult protective conditions
Parallel with late Stage 2. Exercise and MBSR both support autoimmune management and reduce alcohol cravings.
L1
Exercise — autoimmune and alcohol reduction benefit
ScienceModerate exercise reduces autoimmune inflammation, normalises cortisol, and reduces alcohol cravings by 50% in some studies via dopamine pathway regulation. Consistent exercise is one of the most effective alcohol craving reduction tools available without pharmacotherapy.
Action150 min/week moderate aerobic. Start with walking or swimming (gentle on thyroid-fatigued patients). Progress to yoga which has specific L2 evidence for autoimmune conditions. Avoid high-intensity training during autoimmune flares — can worsen inflammation.
Exercise + alcohol cravings: L1 RCT; exercise + autoimmune: L2; exercise + cortisol: L1
L1
MBSR — Mindfulness-Based Stress Reduction (autoimmune + alcohol evidence)
ScienceMBSR has L2 evidence for autoimmune condition management (reduces inflammatory markers and cortisol) and L1 evidence as an alcohol use disorder adjunct — it reduces craving reactivity and emotional dysregulation that drives drinking. An efficient dual-target intervention for Elena's two primary issues.
Action8-week MBSR program. palousemindfulness.com (free, self-paced). Frame as "a tool for your immune system and for managing stress without alcohol." Set expectation: 4–6 weeks to notice craving reduction; 8 weeks for autoimmune benefit.
MBSR + autoimmune: L2; MBSR + alcohol use: L1 meta-analysis (Bowen et al.)
L2
Social connection — build adult protective conditions
SciencePCE 4/7 indicates moderate relational buffer. Social isolation is a known driver of alcohol escalation — addressing loneliness directly reduces alcohol use in some patients. Building trusted adult relationships also activates the biological mechanisms behind PCE protection: co-regulation, safety signalling, reduced allostatic load.
ActionIdentify 2 people in her life she feels most herself around. Encourage regular in-person time. For patients with thin networks, community connection groups (not AA unless patient chooses) provide both social support and a non-alcohol social context.
Bethell 2019; social isolation + alcohol: L2 cohort
